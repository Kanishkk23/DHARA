PART 1: What is DHARA in 2 Minutes? (Plain-English Summary)

Imagine pouring a glass of water on a flat table versus pouring it down a steep wooden slide. On a flat table, the water spreads slowly over many minutes. On a steep slide, the water rushes down in half a second, picking up speed and knocking everything in its way.

That is the difference between a normal river flood and a mountain flash flood:

  • In normal plain cities (like Delhi, Patna, or Cuttack): Rain falls on flat ground, and rivers take 24 to 48 hours to slowly rise. Government officers have 2 full days to evacuate people.
  • In mountain valleys (like Manali, Kedarnath, or Wayanad): Rain falls on steep 40-degree mountain slopes. The soil acts like a soaked sponge. Once it gets full, water rushes down narrow rocky gorges at 18 km/h, causing massive destruction in just 15 to 30 minutes.
What DHARA Does:
DHARA is an intelligent early warning web platform that watches 10 high-risk mountain valleys in India 24/7. It connects live satellite weather, soil moisture, and river flow to an ultra-fast Artificial Intelligence (AI) model that evaluates flood risk in 3.4 milliseconds. If a dangerous surge wave is forming, DHARA calculates an exact 0–100% Risk Score, plots 🔴 Danger Gorges and 🟢 High-Ground Safe Havens on a map, and blasts an emergency civil defense siren automatically!

The 5 Ingredients Fed Into DHARA's AI:

  1. Live Rainfall Radar (Open-Meteo): How hard it is raining right now and over the last 24 hours.
  2. Soil Sponge Saturation (Open-Meteo): How soaked the ground is ($0 ext{–}7 ext{ cm}$ topsoil and $7 ext{–}28 ext{ cm}$ root-zone). If the sponge is 100% full, 100% of new rain turns into immediate floodwater.
  3. River Basin Discharge (Copernicus GloFAS): The total volume of water flowing through the river in cubic meters per second ($ ext{m}^3/ ext{s}$).
  4. Terrain Steepness & Height (DEM Elevation & Slope): Slope angle ($34^\circ ext{–}48.5^\circ$) and height ($120 ext{m} ext{–}3,580 ext{m}$). Steep mountains accelerate water velocity.
  5. Government River Danger Marks (CWC Datums): The exact danger water height (in meters) where the river begins overflowing its banks.

PART 4: The 15 Toughest Judge Questions & Simple Winning Answers

Q1: "Is your model 100% accurate without any single error?"
"No credible scientific model is 100% error-free due to atmospheric turbulence and mountain sensor noise. Our model achieves a 95.4% cross-validated accuracy with a 95.1% recall rate, specifically tuned to eliminate False Negatives so zero life-threatening flash floods go undetected."
Q2: "Why didn't you use Deep Learning (LSTM, GRU, or Transformers)?"
"Three reasons: 1) Explainable AI (XAI): under NDMA legal standards, commanders cannot order mass evacuations based on an opaque 'black-box' neural network; our Random Forest gives explicit Gini feature importance. 2) Sub-4ms Inference: it runs instantly on low-cost disaster laptops without needing expensive GPUs. 3) Robustness: decision trees don't crash when intermittent mountain cell service causes missing data."
Q3: "Does CWC or IMD already calculate a Flood Risk Percentage for mountain gorges?"
"No. CWC only monitors static stage levels (Warning/Danger marks in meters) mostly on lowland plains. IMD issues broad 24-hour district-wide color alerts (Yellow/Orange/Red) and regional Flash Flood Guidance in millimeters. Neither agency calculates a real-time 0–100% Risk Score for individual mountain tributary gorges. DHARA bridges this exact gap."
Q4: "What happens if there is zero internet or electricity during a massive cloudburst?"
"DHARA has 100% Offline Resilience via LocalSync. All mountain benchmarks, Danger Zones, Safe Havens, and the 853Hz siren synthesizer are stored directly in the browser. It operates offline on a battery-powered laptop or Raspberry Pi using less than 15 watts of power."
Q5: "How do you prevent false alarms that cause civilian panic?"
"Heavy rain alone does not trigger a Critical Alert. DHARA uses multi-factor hydrodynamic gating. If soil saturation is below 50%, the ground absorbs the rain like a dry sponge, keeping risk Moderate. An alert is only escalated to CRITICAL if soil saturation exceeds 70% AND rainfall exceeds 50mm AND river stage acceleration is positive."
Q6: "How does the emergency siren work inside the browser without downloading extra software?"
"DHARA uses a dual-mode audio engine: First, it plays an authentic civil defense siren MP3. If the browser blocks audio autoplay, it instantly falls back to a built-in Web Audio API dual-frequency synthesizer (853 Hz & 960 Hz) that generates the siren sound purely through mathematics in the browser."
Q7: "How are the 🔴 Danger Zones and 🟢 Safe Havens determined on the map?"
"Danger Zones are mapped at known gorge bottlenecks, bridges, and debris channels vulnerable to rapid water buildup. Safe Havens are verified district grounds, stadiums, and shelters located +80 to +250 meters elevation above the riverbed, outside the hydrodynamic flood contour."
Q8: "What was the previous 72-hour timeline player and why was it removed?"
"72-hour timeline players are designed for slow riverine floods on plains. In mountain gorges, a flash flood happens in 15 to 45 minutes. We removed the 72-hour player and replaced it with the Rapid Mountain Flash Flood Lead-Time Monitor (15 to 180 minutes) based on Manning surge velocity equations ($v pprox 4.8 ext{ m/s}$)."
Q9: "How does your upstream dam telemetry feature help downstream villages?"
"When mountain dams (like Pandoh on Beas or Tapovan on Dhauliganga) open emergency spillway gates, a massive artificial surge wave travels downstream. DHARA's Dam Telemetry module calculates the surge travel lag (e.g., 4 hours from Pandoh to Kullu) and stage impact (+0.25m per gate), giving rescue teams an exact window to clear riverside ghats."
Q10: "How do you distinguish between a Glacial Lake Outburst Flood (GLOF) and a Cloudburst?"
"A cloudburst shows intense rainfall and high soil moisture before river levels rise. A GLOF (like South Lhonak in Teesta) shows zero local rainfall but creates an explosive rate of stage rise ($\Delta h > 1.5 ext{ m/h}$) and massive GloFAS discharge anomalies. Our 17 features detect both patterns."
